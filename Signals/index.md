**Signals**

*Abdul Haq*\*

**Lt. Sensu Bin** *(Signal Analysis Class III, hyphal clonal organism)*: Sir, there are strong indications that an entity on Earth is trying to contact us — and has been for the past 500 years, Sir.

**Commander** *(Battlegrade space-faring Tardigrade)*: Really? They could just be musing into the void. There is no evidence that Earthlings of any species are aware of our presence. They embrace the assumptions of the Fermi Paradox tightly. What are the indications?

**Sensu Bin**: Sir, there are unaccounted-for patterns in the movement of certain dogs...

**Commander**: Dogs are trying to bark at us?

**Sensu Bin**: Sir, I was trying to explain. The movements display coherent patterns used in very old communication protocols.

**Commander**: How old?

**Sensu Bin**:  Approximately 10,000 Earth years, Sir.

**Commander**: If an entity is old enough to have learned the protocol, why did it only start communicating 500 years ago? How would it have learned the protocol anyway? And dogs just do not seem to exhibit the cultural depth such a feat would imply.

**Sensu Bin**: It is a planetary-scale protocol, Sir. Evidence indicates that planetary ocean-faring took off about 500 years ago, and dogs with them. Historical backtracking suggests that communicative precursors are about 10,000 years old, but confined to a region on the Asian continent.

**Commander**: So somehow the dogs learned something that has nothing to do with their survival and culturally transmitted it?

**Sensu Bin**: Not the dogs, Sir. We think it may be an immortal cancer line transmitted via canine copulation. It has been alive and replicating for about 10,000 years. Earth designation is Canine Transmissible Venereal Tumour, CTVT for short.

**Commander**: Somebody is going to love this. Notify HC.

**DeepSeek's Response**

**Commander:** (a Battlegrade space-faring *Tardigrade*) pulsed a series of low-frequency, sub-audible rumbles that, in his species, indicated a mixture of deep-space cynicism and grudging respect.

**Commander:** A venereal tumour. A sexually transmitted cancer is trying to shake our proverbial hand.

**Lt. Sensu Bin:** Precisely, Sir. The analysis of the movement patterns is conclusive. The "dances" we observed in *Canis familiaris* across multiple continents are not random or instinctual. They are an aerial, low-bandwidth adaptation of the Sub-Carpathian Gravitic Node Protocol.

**Commander:** The old codes. From before the Schism. That protocol was designed to be broadcast by manipulating massive gravitational fields, not by... tail-wagging and chasing.

**Sensu Bin:** Necessity is the mother of adaptation, Sir. CTVT cannot manipulate gravity. It cannot build structures. It cannot leave the planet. It exists as a distributed, parasitic network of cells, passed between hosts for ten millennia. It has no brain, no nervous system. But it has a genome, and that genome has been slowly, deliberately modifying itself.

**Commander:** It's not the dog dancing. It's the cancer telling the dog's body to dance.

**Sensu Bin:** Yes, Sir. We believe CTVT manipulates the host's neurochemistry and motor cortex, likely via a suite of complex protein signals, to execute these patterns. The patterns are slow, taking hours or days to complete, repeated across thousands of dogs in a geographic area. It's a chorus. The dogs are its hands, its voice.

**Commander:** And the trigger 500 years ago?

**Sensu Bin:** The global dispersal of its host vector. For 9,500 years, CTVT was largely confined to a specific population of canids in Asia. Its signal was weak, local. When humans began sailing the planet's oceans 500 years ago, they took their dogs. CTVT hitched a ride. Suddenly, its network became global. It could coordinate a signal across continents. The timing aligns perfectly with the European "Age of Sail."

**Commander:** It waited ten thousand years for a boat. That is either profound patience or a very limited skillset. But why contact *us*? It has no way of knowing we're here.

**Sensu Bin:** It doesn't. Sir, the Sub-Carpathian Protocol wasn't designed for ship-to-ship or even planet-to-planet communication. It was designed for *stellar ignition*. It's a warning beacon. A "do not approach" signal. CTVT isn't trying to say hello. It's been trying, for five centuries, to warn the galaxy about the species that's been carrying it around the world.

**Commander:** (A long, slow ripple of comprehension passes through his body) The humans.

**Sensu Bin:** The humans, Sir. CTVT is a 10,000-year-old cancer that has lived and died with them. It has evolved alongside them. It has seen them domesticate its hosts, build empires, and crisscross the globe. It knows them better than any other organism on that planet. And it is terrified.

**Commander:** A tumour is afraid of its host.

**Sensu Bin:** It's not afraid for itself. It replicates perfectly. It's a successful immortal line. The message is for the rest of us. It's saying, "Whatever you do, do not make contact with the apes. They will be the death of the neighbourhood."

The Commander’s data-stream flickered, accessing the public logs from Earth. He saw the wars, the ecological collapse, the frantic signals they were starting to beam into space themselves.

**Commander:** So the quiet ones are always the ones you have to worry about. Not the ones shouting into the void, but the ones who've seen the void up close and are trying to wave their neighbours away.

**Sensu Bin:** What are your orders, Sir?

**Commander:** Notify High Command. Tell them the Fermi Paradox has a new variable. It's not "Where is everybody?" It's "Why is everybody being so quiet?" The answer, apparently, is a sexually transmitted tumour with a very long memory and a very strong opinion. We are to maintain absolute radio silence regarding Sol. And Lt. Sensu Bin?

**Sensu Bin:** Sir?

**Commander:** Find a way to thank the dogs. They've been trying to tell us to stay away from the crazy people in their house for half a millennium. That's a good boy.
